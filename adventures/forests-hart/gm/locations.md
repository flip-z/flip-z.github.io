# Locations
{: tabindex="-1" }

## Coedmyr village

The investigation hub. Coedmyr was once a remote spiritual pilgrimage destination reached deliberately rather than a place travelers passed through. Pilgrims came for the forest, shrines, vigils, old hunts, and local rites. As the forest declined and those practices faded, visitors stopped making the difficult journey and the village contracted with them.

Important functions include Mayor Elara Venn's office, the Pilgrim's Rest, Maelin Tor's home, Taran Holt's forge, local hunters, and ordinary townsfolk with conflicting beliefs about what remains of the old traditions.

Malformed wildlife can attack near town at dusk early in the investigation. If the party has not already sought out Ewan, he can enter during this encounter and help finish the final creature, providing a natural introduction. If the party later frees the Hart without resolving its condition, a second and more severe attack can demonstrate that the mobile source is worsening the outbreak.

## Pilgrim's Rest

A large inn left over from Coedmyr's pilgrimage era. It has more rooms and tables than the modern village can support. The common room is useful for informal conversations, rumors, and watching villagers react to the Ministry and the unusually rare sight of an elf.

Ewan can return here after the trap-line investigation. If the party later recognizes the need for a bellkeeper, the inn provides a natural place to find and persuade him without making recruitment a mandatory quest.

## Elara Venn's office and home

Elara keeps several years of reports documenting failed crops, sick livestock, unusual wildlife, and local complaints. Her latest chicken story is what finally prompted the Ministry to send a team, but she has no carcass or other physical evidence to prove it happened. The GM does not need to resolve whether she told the complete truth, exaggerated an incident, or invented the story to force a response.

Her records and conversation establish Coedmyr's long decline and provide directions to practical investigation branches such as the north spring, local farms, Taran, Ewan, Maelin, and the overlook.

## North spring

The spring is a useful red herring rather than a major clue node. Animals have avoided the water and Elara reports that it has gone foul.

Investigation finds a badly decomposed animal lodged near the source. Its advanced decay provides a mundane explanation for the local contamination and rewards the trip with a concrete answer.

The wider forest around the spring remains visibly abnormal in ways the carcass does not explain. The useful conclusion is that contaminated water is not a sufficient explanation for the regional outbreak.

## Maelin Tor's home

Maelin lives with or is regularly cared for by his granddaughter Nessa. The house is crowded with books, hunting pieces, pilgrimage keepsakes, bones, carved animals, iron charms, and old hanging ornaments.

Maelin speaks an archaic local dialect rather than cryptic fragments. From his perspective he is speaking normally. Nessa can translate enough to maintain a conversation, but she simplifies, guesses, and occasionally gets details wrong. PCs with suitable Society, Lore, Occultism, or linguistic knowledge can catch old words or correct context.

Maelin's central complaint is practical rather than prophetic: Coedmyr no longer respects the forest, its history, or the obligations that once accompanied pilgrimage. The old tradition joined reverence with stewardship, including hunting, harvest, limits, and other forms of active care.

The Wolf's Tooth is hidden in plain sight among the house's many inherited objects. A careful search can notice it before its importance is known. If the party later mentions Iron-Maw, the old hunt, the unusual shrine implements, or the bell, Maelin recognizes the relevant piece in his collection and retrieves it. He knows its provenance and association with the old hunt, not its exact ritual function.

## Taran Holt's forge

Provides the material recognition and reconstruction path for the Wolf's Tooth. Taran is skeptical and practical. He can identify the relic as forged cold iron designed to mount on a haft and show the party a mundane analogue of the obsolete regional implement.

Once the party has reason to believe the Tooth matters, Taran can supervise a short reconstruction challenge in which the PCs build the ceremonial hunting weapon themselves.

## Pilgrimage overlook

Sera Wynn tends the best-preserved remnant of old Coedmyr pilgrimage culture. The overlook survived longer than harder forest traditions because it remained pleasant and accessible: meditation, exercise, prayer, picnics, views, and simplified ceremonies could continue even as fewer visitors followed the old routes deeper into the woods.

That survival is double-edged. Sera genuinely preserves the heritage and archive, but the overlook's success as a spiritual retreat and tourist attraction helped separate pilgrimage symbolism from the older practices of active stewardship.

The poorly catalogued archive contains records on Coedmyr hunting customs, Iron-Maw, the Hart, traditional tools, spiritual hunts, the spirit trail, and the forgotten shrine. If the party has not already heard of the Hart, the archive can introduce it naturally as part of old local tradition.

Research should establish that Iron-Maw belongs to stories about necessary predation, harvest, culling, and limits within a healthy forest. It should not frame the Hart as something that normally wanders astray or directly explain the present-day solution.

## Spirit trail

An old route through the forest connecting the pilgrimage overlook to the ruined hunting shrine. Weathered wolf carvings mark the path. Careful exploration can reveal it even without complete documentary directions.

## Ewan Rusk's trap line

Local game has become scarce enough that Ewan has neglected distant parts of the line.

The encounter should progress from empty or abandoned traps to a trapped doe whose injury demonstrates the regional pathology. Bone, muscle, and skin have grown badly around damage, infection, debris, or trap metal without restoring normal structure. Examination establishes that the trap itself is ordinary.

One or two minutes before the party reaches the Hart, the surrounding forest becomes increasingly abnormal. Vegetation crowds itself with fresh shoots, fungi and insects become conspicuously abundant, roots and damaged stems produce excessive new growth, and the intensity increases toward the source.

Farther along the line lies the trapped Hart. It is visibly exceptional without relying on antlers: its coat, eyes, proportions, movement, and presence distinguish it immediately from mundane wildlife. It appears distressed and trapped rather than merely malformed.

A 25-foot aura around the Hart is actively hazardous. Entering or remaining inside it during structured play calls for a DC 32 Fortitude save and causes concrete uncontrolled biological responses in living creatures: inflamed scars, thickened tissue around small cuts, swelling at old injuries, or painful nail and hair growth. Environmental warning signs extend well beyond the mechanical radius.

Freeing the Hart is valid but makes the source mobile and intensifies subsequent outbreaks.

## Corrupted forest

Contains malformed fauna, contaminated waterways, evidence of the long ecological decline, and the routes used during the final pursuit.

Describe the corruption physically rather than explaining its theme for the players: redundant growth, distorted bone, wounds engulfing debris, excessive tissue, crowded vegetation, abnormal fungal or insect abundance, and biological processes continuing without healthy organization.

## Ruined hunting shrine

The final ritual destination. Clearing growth and debris exposes the bell and carvings showing distinct bellkeeper and hunter roles. Hunter figures carry unusual long implements with broad striking roots and pronounced points.

An ordinary strike produces only a momentary pale aura regardless of how hard the bell is hit. Greater force makes a louder sound, not a stronger ritual effect. The Wolf's Tooth produces a qualitatively cleaner and stronger resonance, revealing it as the correct striker.

During the final rite, the active boundary excludes ordinary corrupted wildlife while allowing the called Hart to enter. Inside the aura, corruption is driven out of the Hart and suspended visibly around the shrine perimeter rather than destroyed. Interrupting the bell allows that corruption to press back inward and creates openings for malformed creatures to breach the boundary.
