# Forest's Hart
{: tabindex="-1" }

Forest's Hart is an investigative folklore adventure for four 10th-level Pathfinder Second Edition characters. The Ministry sends the party to the remote village of Coedmyr after years of increasingly strange reports finally culminate in an absurd claim: a slaughtered chicken got up from a kitchen counter and killed a neighbor's cat.

The case should initially permit mundane explanations. Coedmyr is declining, the forest is unhealthy, local institutions are failing, and several individual problems have ordinary causes. The supernatural explanation becomes persuasive only as the party accumulates combinations that should not physically coexist or uses appropriate spiritual investigation.

Clues should remain mutually consistent without converting into an automatic GM explanation. The party can enter the finale with an incomplete or mistaken theory.

Early evidence should remain plausibly natural. Coedmyr is a shrinking settlement inside a sick, neglected forest; players should be able to wonder whether the apparent mystery is simply ecological collapse, disease, abandonment, or the forest reclaiming the town. The supernatural case becomes persuasive through physically impossible combinations, abnormal timing, and earned spiritual observations.

The adventure's human problem is broader than a forgotten ritual. Coedmyr once cared for the forest through distributed habits, labor, observation, and shared responsibility. The old rite was one emergency procedure inside that system. The supernatural crisis became catastrophic because the community no longer had the coverage, coordination, or working knowledge that would once have brought unusual conditions to attention early.

The Ministry can contain the immediate supernatural emergency. It cannot repair the ordinary damage, depopulation, or lost institutions that allowed the emergency to grow unnoticed.

## GM Background — The Disturbance

The present crisis is not an invading corruption substance and not a simple excess of life or vitality.

The Hart belongs to a broader continent-wide body of spiritual folklore. It represents renewal, succession, recovery, and replacement after loss. The Wolf represents predation, selection, limits, endings, and removal. The River Turtle represents persistence, circulation, water, soil, and long-duration systems. The Bird represents movement, migration, dispersal, seasonal change, and passage between places.

These are not a closed Coedmyr pantheon. Maelin, Nessa, and old records should contain unrelated stories and regional disagreements so the Wolf and Hart do not read as a convenient two-part puzzle.

The Hart can take physical form. A manifestation is mortal by design and is not the totality of the greater spirit. In Coedmyr's old tradition, a Hart manifestation eventually reached the end of its season and was taken in a ritual hunt associated with the Wolf. Human hunters enacted the Wolf's role through the Wolf's Tooth.

The present Hart manifestation reached one of Ewan Rusk's neglected traps and remained physically anchored beyond the point when its passage should have completed. Other hunters have also shortened routes or stopped coordinating responsibilities as game and population declined, but Ewan specifically left traps unattended. His trap is the incidental accelerant that allows the supernatural failure to become acute, not the cause of Coedmyr's long deterioration.

That interruption does not cause the Hart to emit magical corruption. Instead, normally connected natural processes around the manifestation increasingly fail to resolve cleanly into one another.

Growth, repair, inflammation, decomposition, predation, migration, crowding, and recovery still occur, but at contradictory rates, in the wrong order, or without clean transition into whatever should follow. This is the underlying **disturbance** or **disjunction**.

An older Coedmyr with more people routinely moving through the forest, checking routes, watching game, and passing unusual observations back toward the overlook would probably have found the trapped Hart before the condition reached this scale. That does not make ordinary neglect a supernatural cause. The Hart's entrapment causes the acute crisis; the village's diminished stewardship lets it become catastrophic.

Villagers may describe malformed creatures or places as corrupted. GM-facing material should not imply that corruption is a separate substance radiated by the Hart.

## FH-00 — Ministry Arrival

The Ministry has received increasingly worried annual reports from Mayor Elara Venn for years. Coedmyr is remote, economically marginal, and already known to be deteriorating, so those reports never justified committing a field team.

Elara's latest report contains one detail strange enough to change that calculation: she claims that a slaughtered and dressed chicken got off her kitchen counter and killed a neighbor's cat.

There is no carcass, no preserved evidence, and no independent proof. The Ministry does not dispatch because it accepts the story. The team is being sent to determine whether something extraordinary is happening or whether Elara's future reports can be safely deprioritized.

The approach into Coedmyr should establish a visibly unhealthy montane forest before the party reaches town. Do not make it overtly magical. Dead and crowded growth, damaged trees, poor understory, fungal activity, washed-out paths, weak animal sign, and abandoned maintenance should support several mundane explanations.

The first villager encounter should usually be Taran Holt at his forge. He is skeptical of the Ministry's necessity but not obstructive. He knows Elara has been sending letters for years and can point the team toward her.

Elara can supply three concrete branches without knowing their shared cause:

- the north spring has gone foul or is being avoided by animals;
- crops and livestock have been failing in inconsistent ways;
- aggressive or malformed wildlife has been seen close to town.

She can also explain Coedmyr's history. It was never a crossroads. People came here deliberately for spiritual pilgrimage. The decline was gradual enough that residents adapted year by year while those with money or reason to leave did so.

Her records can show the village losing practical capacity over time: fewer hunters covering less ground, deferred repairs, unresolved complaints, and more residents leaving. Present these as history and local circumstance rather than as an explicit clue to the supernatural case.

### North spring

The spring is a useful red herring rather than a major clue node.

The water appears clear, but investigation finds a badly decomposed animal lodged at or immediately beside the source. Its advanced decay provides a mundane and satisfying explanation for the local contamination.

Nothing supernatural is required to explain the spring itself. The carcass has simply remained there long enough to foul the source.

That does not explain the broader condition of the forest. Vegetation around the spring can still show the same regional abnormalities visible elsewhere: overcrowded shoots, distorted growth around damage, fungal activity appearing at unusual times, or other biological processes occurring without healthy organization.

The useful conclusion is narrow: the spring itself is not a sufficient explanation for Coedmyr's larger problem.

### Dusk incursion

Around dusk, preferably as the party returns toward town or settles in for the night, malformed wildlife attacks near Coedmyr.

The creatures should be recognizably natural animals made dangerous by contradictory biological processes rather than fantasy mutants with wholly new anatomy. Wounds may be overgrown with tissue that never restored function. Infection and repair may coexist. Bone growth may stabilize an injury in the wrong position.

If the party has not already sought Ewan Rusk out, he can enter late in the fight and help finish the last creature. This creates a natural introduction without turning him into a quest-giver.

## FH-01 — Maelin Tor and Nessa

Maelin Tor is the oldest surviving keeper of local language, stories, inherited objects, and practical memory. He is not an oracle and should not be performed as cryptic.

He speaks plainly in an archaic Coedmyr dialect. His granddaughter Nessa translates, but her translation is imperfect. She simplifies, guesses, argues with him, and occasionally mistranslates older ecological or ritual terms.

PCs with appropriate Society, Lore, Occultism, linguistic knowledge, or another convincing basis can catch recognizable words, context, or errors.

Maelin's central argument is cultural and practical. Coedmyr once treated the forest as something people were obligated to care for, not simply admire. Pilgrimage involved work, restraint, hunting, harvest, maintenance, and knowledge of place as well as prayer and ceremony.

The pilgrimage overlook once housed a keeper who watched conditions, preserved working knowledge, and coordinated duties distributed across hunters, farmers, craftspeople, shrine tenders, and other residents. The keeper was not a lone priest performing magical maintenance on the forest.

Modern Coedmyr has kept fragments while losing much of the network that connected them. Maelin can be bitter about this without possessing a complete supernatural explanation for the current outbreak.

His view is not the adventure's official verdict. Taran can reasonably argue that a shrinking village could not keep every old obligation alive. Elara can point out that Coedmyr spent years asking for help while losing people and money. Sera sincerely preserves what she was taught without knowing the deeper institution behind it. Let those disagreements stand.

His stories should range broadly. The Hart, Wolf, River Turtle, and Bird all appear in old traditions. Regional accounts disagree about names, relationships, and exact forms. Turtle and Bird stories should include details that are culturally useful but irrelevant to this case.

### Wolf's Tooth

The Wolf's Tooth is hidden in plain sight among Maelin's inherited objects.

A careful search can notice the dark cold-iron piece before its significance is known. It resembles a broad tooth or hooked striking head but is clearly forged rather than biological.

If the party later mentions the Wolf, the old hunt, unusual shrine implements, or the bell, Maelin recognizes the relevant object and retrieves it. The relic cannot be permanently missed because later discoveries naturally create a reason to return.

Maelin knows the Tooth is associated with the Wolf and the old hunt. He does not know that it is the correct bell striker or the complete current ritual procedure.

## FH-02 — Taran Holt and Reconstruction

Taran is a practical skeptic. He recognizes that the Wolf's Tooth is forged cold iron rather than a literal tooth. Its shape and mounting points indicate that it was part of a longer obsolete ceremonial implement.

He knows ordinary Wolf folklore but not the lost ritual. His useful contribution is material interpretation.

Taran also provides a useful counterargument to Maelin. Old customs required people, labor, and money. As Coedmyr contracted, residents stopped doing things they could no longer justify or sustain. He can acknowledge that useful practices were lost without agreeing that the answer is simple cultural repentance.

Once the party has reason to believe the Tooth matters, Taran can help reconstruct the full implement. The PCs participate rather than handing the problem to him.

### Reconstruction challenge

Run the reconstruction as a short skill challenge using Crafting and any other convincing contributions.

Overlook research about traditional tools can provide an advantage or lower difficulty. Physical inspection of carvings, old haft fittings, or regional analogues can also help.

The weapon is never lost to failed rolls. Results determine time and finish:

- strong success produces a sound, balanced reconstruction;
- mixed success produces a usable but awkward or fragile implement;
- poor success costs additional time, materials, or later maintenance.

The exact implement form remains a design question. It should allow the Tooth to function both as the bell striker and as the final ritual weapon.

## FH-03 — Overlook, Spirit Trail, and Shrine

Sera Wynn tends the old pilgrimage overlook and its poorly catalogued archive.

She apprenticed under the previous caretaker and later inherited responsibility for the site. Her training preserved surviving ceremonies, stories, habits, and a sincere concern for the forest, but not a complete understanding of the older institution that once operated from the overlook.

The overlook was historically Coedmyr's spiritual center and almost literally a watchtower over the forest. Old records can reveal that a keeper once watched conditions, maintained records, and coordinated work distributed across the community: hunters covering routes and game, residents tending shrines and water sources, craftspeople maintaining tools, and others reporting unusual changes.

That institutional history should be something the party reconstructs from records and context, not knowledge Sera simply hands them.

The overlook survived longer than the harder forest traditions because it remained the pleasant, accessible part of pilgrimage culture. Visitors could meditate, exercise, drink tea, pray, picnic, admire the view, and participate in simplified ceremonies without learning the older routes and obligations deeper in the forest.

Sera genuinely values and preserves Coedmyr's heritage. In her younger years she also benefited financially from the retreat economy that kept the site alive. Those facts are compatible. Skeptical PCs may initially read her as an earnest romantic preservationist before they understand how much of the older tradition has been lost.

The failure here is not that Sera commercialized a sacred site. It is that the surviving caretaker tradition retained only fragments of a much larger system.

### Overlook research

The archive can introduce several ideas without giving the solution:

- **The Wolf** appears in old material about predation, harvest, culling, limits, and endings.
- **The Hart** appears in the same broad body of tradition. If the party has not already heard the name, this is a natural place to introduce it.
- **The River Turtle** and **Bird** appear in unrelated records, prayers, seasonal customs, travel traditions, or stewardship practices, making clear that the Hart and Wolf are part of a broader spiritual ecology rather than a two-part puzzle.
- Old **spiritual hunts** were ceremonial expressions of active stewardship and ecological balance, not ordinary subsistence hunts.
- Keeper records include route checks, water observations, harvest limits, game notes, shrine maintenance, and reports of unusual conditions.
- Records show the keeper coordinating responsibilities rather than personally performing all of them.
- Old tool diagrams and hunting accounts can support later reconstruction of the Wolf's Tooth implement.

Records should deepen the party's understanding of what Coedmyr once did, not simply what it believed. They should still stop short of directly stating what must happen to the present Hart.

Old records or exploration reveal the **spirit trail**, marked by weathered wolf carvings, leading from the overlook to a ruined hunting shrine.

### Spirit-trail forest hook

The trail itself shows uneven abandonment. Sections near the overlook may remain passable because Sera still walks or clears them occasionally. Deeper sections disappear under deadfall, erosion, and growth.

Do not frame that condition as a clue with a required thematic conclusion. It is simply what an old route looks like after years of shrinking use and maintenance.

If the party has not yet meaningfully entered the forest or uncovered Ewan's neglected trap line, the spirit trail can offer one additional hook without creating a new investigation node.

Use one of the following:

- abnormal animal cries from deeper woods;
- malformed tracks crossing the route;
- an abandoned or damaged trap;
- signs of old trapping infrastructure disappearing beneath growth.

Following the hook intersects Ewan's existing trap-line content.

### Ruined hunting shrine

The shrine is overgrown and partially buried but structurally usable once cleared.

Clearing growth and debris exposes a bell, carved figures, and enough working space to experiment.

Any ordinary strike produces a weak note and a sub-round pale aura that briefly suppresses the local disturbance. Hitting harder increases volume but does not strengthen the effect.

Striking the bell with the Wolf's Tooth produces a qualitatively different response: a dramatically cleaner resonance and a larger, stronger aura. Without continued ringing the effect still fades quickly. This reveals that the Tooth is the correct striker without explaining the entire rite.

Carvings show a recognizable keeper or cantor at the bell while hunters depart carrying long implements. Other villagers are present around the rite, but their exact tasks are not legible. The image suggests a communal ceremony without functioning as an instruction diagram.

The party can reasonably infer that the bell was maintained while the hunters operated elsewhere. Once they discover that the correct response requires sustained ringing, the practical need for continued shrine operation should be obvious.

No particular villager is established as the canonical bellkeeper. The rite establishes only that the shrine must keep operating while the hunt proceeds; exactly how the party maintains that operation remains part of the unfinished final encounter design.

The shrine does **not** tell them what is hunted, that the bell calls the Hart, why the hunt was necessary, or that the Hart must die.

## FH-04 — Ewan Rusk's Trap Line

Ewan Rusk is an experienced hunter who has stopped checking distant portions of his trap line because local game has dwindled. He does not know the Hart reached one of those unattended traps or that this corresponds with the recent escalation.

Ewan is part of a broader contraction in local hunting effort. Other hunters have shortened routes or stopped coordinating coverage as Coedmyr and its game populations declined. Ewan's specific failure is more serious: he left live traps unattended.

That distinction matters. He is not responsible for Coedmyr's collapse, but his neglected trap happens to be the incidental accelerant that turns a wider vulnerability into the acute supernatural crisis.

Ewan supplies practical forest knowledge. He does not recognize the Hart through old lore and should not explain the supernatural meaning of the rite.

The party can reach the trap line through Ewan or by following signs away from the spirit trail.

### Neglected traps

Start with empty or abandoned traps and evidence that portions of the line have not been checked in some time.

The scene should establish practical negligence without making Ewan cartoonishly irresponsible. He stopped walking distant ground because the line stopped producing enough game to justify the effort and then failed to remove every trap he left behind.

### Malformed doe

A trapped doe provides the clearest physical evidence before the Hart.

The trap itself is mundane. The injury is not.

Bone remodeling continues around the jaws without restoring alignment. Inflammation and infection remain active beside layers of new tissue. Skin or muscle repair incorporates debris or proceeds in incompatible directions. The amount and timing of repair do not fit the age of the wound.

Do not tell players that the doe is "unable to finish dying." Let them observe contradictory processes.

The investigator can reconstruct timing and establish that the trap is ordinary. The druid or another suitable spiritual specialist can establish that the animal's natural processes are failing to resolve cleanly and that a wider spiritual disturbance is present.

Neither result identifies the Hart or supplies the ritual answer.

### Approaching the Hart

Environmental abnormalities intensify one or two minutes before the party reaches the source.

Growth, fungi, insects, decay, wound response, and other biological activity occur at increasingly contradictory rates or in the wrong sequence.

The Hart itself should be unmistakably exceptional without relying only on antlers. Its coat, eyes, proportions, movement, and presence distinguish it immediately from mundane wildlife. It should look trapped and exhausted rather than grotesquely malformed.

A 25-foot aura around the Hart is actively hazardous. Entering or remaining inside during structured play calls for a DC 32 Fortitude save.

Effects should be concrete changes in the exposed character's own body: inflamed scars, excessive tissue around cuts, swelling around old injuries, painful nail or hair growth, or similar maladaptive repair and growth.

The aura is not emitted corruption. It is the strongest local expression of the disjunction around a manifestation that has remained anchored beyond its proper transition.

The party may experiment with the Wolf's Tooth, attack the Hart, leave it trapped, or free it. None of these ordinary interventions resolves its condition.

Looking backward through the investigation, the party may conclude that a village with broader forest coverage and intact working knowledge would probably have found this extraordinary trapped animal sooner. That inference should emerge from accumulated context rather than being presented as the explicit lesson of the node.

### Freeing the Hart early

Freeing the Hart is a valid player choice and should not be blocked. Once freed, the strongest focus of the disturbance becomes mobile. Outbreaks around Coedmyr intensify and the Hart must later be reacquired during the ritual hunt.

A second, more severe wildlife attack near town can demonstrate this escalation and create urgency without forcing the party directly into the finale.

## FH-05 — Assemble a Response

Once the party understands that the shrine must remain active while the Hart is pursued, the problem expands beyond the actions of the four investigators.

Do not turn this into a mandatory recruitment checklist. The Ministry can attempt the rite alone. Villager participation should show that some people in Coedmyr are willing to recover pieces of the old tradition and work together again.

A few named villagers plus interested locals are enough. Earlier interactions determine who is willing to show up.

Do not assign fixed mechanical jobs yet. The final encounter mechanics remain unfinished, so Sera, Ewan, Taran, Nessa, Elara, and unnamed villagers should not be locked into canonical combat functions in advance.

Their participation should eventually buy capability, coverage, resilience, or knowledge that the PCs would otherwise have to supply themselves, but the exact benefits belong to the final encounter design.

No particular NPC is a required bellkeeper, organizer, tracker, translator, or defender.

The point is not that outsiders are magically forbidden from performing the rite. The point is that local willingness to participate can briefly recreate the cooperative character of the old tradition.

## What the Party May Know Before the Finale

A well-prepared party can reasonably have assembled some or all of the following:

- Coedmyr's forest is suffering both ordinary long-term decline and a newer impossible disturbance;
- the Hart is a continent-wide spiritual figure associated with renewal and succession;
- the Wolf follows the Hart in some old traditions and represents predation, limits, and endings;
- the current Hart manifestation is physically trapped and has endured beyond its proper season;
- Ewan's unattended trap is the incidental accelerant for the recent supernatural escalation;
- the Wolf's Tooth is human-made cold iron associated with the old hunt;
- the Tooth was built as part of a hunting implement;
- the Tooth correctly activates the shrine bell;
- the old rite involved continued shrine operation while hunters departed;
- the overlook once housed a keeper who coordinated observation, maintenance, hunting, and ritual work distributed across the village;
- Coedmyr's ordinary decline reduced the coverage and shared knowledge that might once have caught the Hart's condition earlier.

These facts support theories. They do not automatically produce the correct one. The party can enter the finale uncertain about why the hunt exists, exactly what the bell does, or what final action will be required.

## FH-FINAL — Ritual Hunt

The final encounter mechanics remain intentionally unfinished. Preserve the following narrative structure and communal payoff when building them.

### Beginning the rite

Repeatedly striking the shrine bell with the reconstructed Wolf's Tooth creates a sustained pale aura. After the bell rings for a short time, the party notices disturbances moving through the forest and eventually hears or glimpses the Hart responding.

The bell **calls** the Hart but does not compel it to approach. This is the moment the old carvings become legible as procedure: the shrine must remain active while hunters go after the Hart.

The rite does not require Sera or any other specific NPC to operate the bell. Exactly how the party keeps the shrine active while pursuing the Hart remains part of the unfinished encounter design; villager help may make that easier, but the narrative does not assume a dedicated bellkeeper.

If villagers agreed to participate, show them taking part according to whatever mechanical structure is eventually designed. The shrine should feel briefly inhabited as a working place again rather than as an abandoned puzzle room operated only by outsiders.

### Phase 1 — Pursuit

Use a modified PF2e Chase structure. The party tracks, pressures, and drives the Hart back toward the active shrine. Obstacles can include disordered terrain, waterways, malformed vegetation, losing contact, and creatures moving in the Hart's wake.

Attack rolls, skills, spells, and appropriate equipment can all overcome obstacles.

Failures do not end the hunt. They increase **breach pressure**. Each failure represents the party falling farther behind the Hart and gives problems in its wake more opportunity to reach and interfere with the shrine response.

If the Hart was freed earlier, reacquiring it adds an additional obstacle.

Villager participation can modify the chase once exact mechanics are designed, but NPCs should not replace the PCs or solve primary obstacles for them.

### Phase 2 — Shrine and containment

The Hart can cross the active boundary because the rite specifically calls it home. Ordinary malformed wildlife cannot cross while the bell is maintained.

Inside the active aura, the shrine temporarily restores enough order around the Hart for the disjunction to separate from its physical manifestation.

Represent the remaining disturbance as targetable manifestations, lesions, growths, environmental objectives, or other encounter components attached to the Hart and shrine. The exact shrine-phase rules remain TBD.

The Hart remains an active threat while distressed. It can charge, kick, reposition, attempt to escape the shrine, and disrupt whoever is maintaining the rite.

When the bell stops, the boundary flickers. The surrounding disjunction presses back inward and malformed creatures outside can breach the perimeter. Restoring the bell pushes the effect outward again, but anything that crossed remains inside and must be dealt with.

Breach pressure from the chase determines the opening state. A party that arrives behind the Hart may find the shrine response already under pressure or one or more malformed creatures inside the boundary.

The core combat loop remains:

**Maintain bell → stabilize/expose Hart → Hart disrupts response → boundary breaches → restore response → contain the breach → continue.**

Villager participation should eventually alter secondary pressure according to the relationships established earlier. Exact roles and benefits remain undefined until the encounter itself is designed.

Do not let villagers solve the primary encounter. The party still has to pursue the Hart, survive its disturbance, interpret what the rite is doing, and make the final decision.

### Phase 3 — The Lucid Hart

Once the shrine has sufficiently separated the manifestation from the surrounding disjunction, the Hart becomes coherent, lucid, and mortal.

It may communicate briefly.

The Hart can convey that this shape has endured beyond its season and that what should have followed never came. It may identify the Wolf as the figure that traditionally follows or takes the Hart.

It should not directly order the party to kill it or explain the mechanical solution.

The PCs should still have to interpret the Tooth, the old hunt, the Wolf/Hart relationship, and the present condition.

### Complete the Passage

Once the Hart is fully exposed inside the active aura, a character adjacent to it and holding the Wolf's Tooth can spend 1 action to complete the rite.

No final check is required.

The physical Hart manifestation dies. The greater spiritual Hart is not destroyed.

The rite is framed as completing the expected passage of a mortal manifestation rather than killing a god.

### If the party leaves the Hart alive

If the party refuses to kill the exposed Hart, the bell can maintain the ordered state only temporarily.

The separated disjunction remains suspended at the boundary rather than disappearing. The Hart cannot be preserved indefinitely in this state.

As the shrine weakens or the party stops maintaining the rite, the disturbance begins returning to the Hart and the broader crisis resumes.

The players should receive enough visible evidence that continued preservation is failing. This should not function as a hidden moral gotcha.

## Resolution

With the Hart manifestation dead, Coedmyr does not become instantly lush. The acute supernatural disturbance ends. Impossible growth, malformed healing, and escalating wildlife behavior subside as ordinary ecological relationships become possible again.

The ordinary damage remains. Depleted game does not immediately return. Diseased or damaged trees remain damaged. Neglected routes and water sources are still neglected. The population and economy are still diminished. The Ministry has contained the extraordinary emergency; it has not restored Coedmyr.

If villagers participated in the finale, the scene can provide evidence that some local interest in the old practices has returned without pretending the system has already been rebuilt. A few people may agree to walk routes again, maintain a shrine, compare observations, or help Sera preserve material that now has clearer context.

The final local question is whether Coedmyr chooses to become a community that watches and cares for the forest again. That decision belongs to Coedmyr, not the Ministry.

Participants in the finale are at genuine risk. No NPC death should be scripted.

If the party is defeated, abandons the rite, or repeatedly leaves the Hart alive after understanding the consequence, the disturbance continues to worsen and can become a larger regional threat.
